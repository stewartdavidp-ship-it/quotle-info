Who recorded it first

Hooked on a Feeling

Not a Blue Swede original — a 1968 B. J. Thomas single on Scepter

Because of “Reservoir Dogs” and the “Guardians of the Galaxy” trailers, most listeners assume “Hooked on a Feeling” began with Blue Swede in 1974. It did not. Mark James wrote it and B. J. Thomas recorded it at American Sound Studio in Memphis in 1968, produced by Chips Moman, with Reggie Young’s electric sitar as its signature; Scepter Records issued it as a single that reached No. 5 on the Billboard Hot 100. The chanted intro everyone imitates is not on the original at all — it was added by Jonathan King in 1971 and inherited by Blue Swede.

Setting the record straight

Blue Swede’s 1974 single is the version almost everyone can hum, and film soundtracks have kept it that way. But it is a cover of a cover’s idea, sitting on top of a 1968 American hit.

Context

Why the cover swallowed the original

The unusual thing about this one is that the original was not obscure. B. J. Thomas’s record was a hit — No. 5 on the Billboard Hot 100, pulled from his Scepter album “On My Way,” cut at American Sound Studio in Memphis with Chips Moman producing and Reggie Young playing the electric sitar line that gives the record its shimmer. It was not overlooked. It was overwritten.

What overwrote it was an addition nobody in Memphis had anything to do with. In 1971 the English singer and producer Jonathan King recorded the song with a chanted vocal introduction of his own invention, and reached No. 23 on the UK singles chart. Blue Swede kept that chant, added brass and a heavier beat, and in April 1974 went to No. 1 in the United States. The hook that now defines the song in most people’s heads is therefore not the songwriter’s, not the original artist’s, and not even the hit act’s — and once film soundtracks locked onto that arrangement, the 1968 record had no way back.

The chronology, step by step
  • 1968 — Mark James writes the song; B. J. Thomas records it at American Sound Studio in Memphis with Chips Moman producing and Reggie Young on electric sitar.
  • 1968 — Scepter Records issues it as a single (catalogue SCE12230), backed with “I’ve Been Down This Road Before”; it also appears on the Scepter album “On My Way.”
  • 1969 — the Thomas single peaks at No. 5 on the Billboard Hot 100.
  • 1971 — Jonathan King records a version with a chanted introduction of his own devising, reaching No. 23 in the UK.
  • 1973 — Blue Swede, fronted by Björn Skifs, record the song and release it in Sweden in May, keeping King’s chant.
  • 1974 — EMI issues it in the United States in February; it reaches No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in April.
  • 1992 and 2014 — the Blue Swede recording appears on the “Reservoir Dogs” soundtrack and then in the “Guardians of the Galaxy” campaign, cementing that arrangement as the version most listeners know.
The original was a top-five hit. It still lost.

The original recording artist

B. J. Thomas

1942-2021 · Hugo, Oklahoma · Scepter Records

Billy Joe Thomas cut “Hooked on a Feeling” at Chips Moman’s American Sound Studio in Memphis in 1968, and it became his second top-ten hit and second million-seller, reaching No. 5 on the Billboard Hot 100 after his 1966 No. 8 cover of “I’m So Lonesome I Could Cry.” He followed it with “Raindrops Keep Fallin’ on My Head,” an Academy Award winner and later a Grammy Hall of Fame inductee. From the mid-1970s he moved largely into contemporary Christian music while still performing the pop hits. He died in 2021.

BS

Popularly credited as the original

Blue Swede

Active 1973-1979 · Sweden · fronted by Björn Skifs

Blue Swede formed in 1973 around singer Björn Skifs, and their 1973 recording of the song, issued in Sweden in May 1973 and in the United States through EMI in February 1974, went to No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in April 1974 and the top ten in Australia (No. 4), Canada (No. 2) and New Zealand (No. 7). Theirs is a genuinely inspired arrangement — brass, a hard backbeat and the chanted vocal hook — and Thomas himself called it a great production that deserved its success. The only error in the popular memory is chronological: the record is a cover, and its most famous element was borrowed from a third artist.

MJ

The songwriter

Mark James

1940-2024 · born Francis Rodney Zambon, Houston, Texas

Mark James wrote “Hooked on a Feeling” for the Memphis sessions that produced some of the era’s most durable pop records. In the same period he wrote “Suspicious Minds,” a No. 1 hit for Elvis Presley, and he later co-wrote “Always on My Mind,” which won Grammy Awards for Song of the Year and Best Country Song in 1983. He was inducted into the Nashville Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2015 and died in 2024.
