Who recorded it first

Beyond the Sea

Not a Bobby Darin original — a December 1945 Roland Gerbeau recording of Charles Trenet’s “La Mer”

Bobby Darin’s swinging Atco single is so completely his that most listeners assume he originated it. He did not: the song began life as “La Mer,” written by the French chansonnier Charles Trenet, and the first recording of it was made at the end of 1945 by the French singer Roland Gerbeau — with the singer Renée Lebas named alongside him as jointly first, and French sources dating the session to November or December — before Trenet himself got round to recording it, in March 1946. Jack Lawrence later wrote an English lyric under the title “Beyond the Sea,” and it was Harry James and His Orchestra who cut that English version first, on 22 December 1947. Darin’s recording was not made until 24 August 1958 and was not released as a single until January 1960.

The paper trail

The first recording of “La Mer” was made by Roland Gerbeau at the end of 1945 — and, as Wikipedia notes ↗, the song’s author Charles Trenet did not record it until the following year (19 or 25 March 1946, for Columbia). The exact date and the sole credit are both hedged by our sources: French Wikipedia’s biography of Gerbeau ↗ puts the session in “November or December 1945” and says he was the first to record it jointly with the singer Renée Lebas; the French article on “La Mer” says only “the end of 1945,” and English Wikipedia says December.

The physical evidence survives: a Sofradi 78, catalogue S. 1273, digitised from the shellac by the Great 78 Project ↗. The label credits the orchestra to Pierre Guillermin, though the English Wikipedia article on “La Mer” ↗ attributes the backing to Jo Bouillon’s orchestra — the two accounts disagree on that detail.

The English title arrived with Jack Lawrence’s lyric, which recast a song about the sea as a love song; Harry James and His Orchestra recorded it first ↗, on 22 December 1947.

Darin’s take was recorded on 24 August 1958 and held back until Atco released it in January 1960 — the version that, for most listeners, is the only one.

Fact-check

Right song, wrong originator

Bobby Darin’s 1960 Atco single is so thoroughly identified with him that it is routinely described as a Darin original. It is a cover of a cover — of an English rewrite of a French chanson that had already been on record for years.

The beliefCover, 1958 recording / 1960 single

Bobby Darin

Darin recorded it on 24 August 1958; Atco released it as a single (Atco 6158) in January 1960, where it reached No. 6 on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 8 in the UK. Superb record, later arrival.

The originalFirst recording, December 1945

Roland Gerbeau

The French singer Roland Gerbeau made the first recording of “La Mer,” issued on a Sofradi 78 (S. 1273) — before the song’s own author recorded it. French Wikipedia dates the session to November or December 1945 and credits the singer Renée Lebas as jointly first.

The writerComposition and English lyric

Charles Trenet, with Jack Lawrence

Trenet wrote “La Mer” (music credited to Trenet and Albert Lasry) and recorded it himself in 1946. Jack Lawrence wrote the English lyric that became “Beyond the Sea,” recorded first by Harry James and His Orchestra on 22 December 1947.

The first voice on this melody was Roland Gerbeau’s — jointly with Renée Lebas, French sources say — at the end of 1945: a full French chanson called “La Mer,” not a love song, and not Bobby Darin’s.

Context

Why the cover swallowed the original

The English-language world never really met “La Mer” as a French song. It met a rewritten one. Jack Lawrence’s lyric kept the title’s sense of distance but changed the subject entirely — Trenet was singing about the sea, and the English version is about someone waiting on the other side of it. A love song travels further than a seascape, and once Harry James and His Orchestra cut the first English version in December 1947, the French original was effectively a different piece of music to American ears.

Then came Darin. His reading is fast, brassy and gleeful where Gerbeau’s Sofradi 78 is a stately French dance-band record, and it arrived on Atco in January 1960 into a market that was buying exactly that kind of swagger. It charted on both sides of the Atlantic. Roland Gerbeau’s version had no English lyric, no chart, and no route into American radio at all — so the record that reached everyone became, in the popular account, the record that started it.

The chronology, step by step
  • Charles Trenet writes “La Mer” (music credited to Trenet and Albert Lasry).
  • December 1945: Roland Gerbeau makes the first recording of the song. It is issued on a Sofradi 78, catalogue S. 1273, matrix S2394, backed with “Amour Bleu.”
  • 1946: Trenet records his own version, on Columbia. The song is published the same year by Les Editions Raoul Breton.
  • Jack Lawrence writes an English lyric and retitles it “Beyond the Sea,” recasting the song as a love song.
  • 22 December 1947: Harry James and His Orchestra make the first recording of the English “Beyond the Sea.”
  • 24 August 1958: Bobby Darin records his version.
  • January 1960: Atco releases Darin’s single (Atco 6158). It reaches No. 6 on the Billboard Hot 100, No. 15 R&B, No. 7 in Canada, No. 8 in the UK.
Darin made it famous. Gerbeau made it first.
RG

The original recording artist

Roland Gerbeau

1919-2012 · French singer-songwriter

Roland Gerbeau was born in Vincennes on 12 November 1919 and died in Saint-Sébastien-de-Morsent on 28 December 2012. He is remembered chiefly for one thing: in December 1945 he made the first recording of Charles Trenet’s “La Mer,” ahead of the composer’s own version. The disc survives — a Sofradi 78 (S. 1273), digitised from the shellac by the Great 78 Project ↗, with the orchestra credited on the label to Pierre Guillermin. Nothing else in his career travelled remotely as far as the song he happened to sing first.

BD

Popularly credited as the original

Bobby Darin

1936-1973 · American singer, songwriter and actor

Born Walden Robert Cassotto, Darin moved from rock and roll novelty hits into big-band swing and made the definitive English-language reading of this song. He recorded it on 24 August 1958; Atco issued it as a single (Atco 6158) in January 1960, and it reached No. 6 on the Billboard Hot 100, No. 15 on the US R&B chart, No. 7 in Canada and No. 8 in the UK. The arrangement is his own territory entirely — the only error in the popular account is chronological.

CT

The songwriter

Charles Trenet

1913-2001 · French singer-songwriter

Trenet wrote both words and music for close to a thousand songs across a career of more than sixty years, among them “Boum!” and “Nationale 7.” “La Mer” is his most travelled composition; Albert Lasry is credited alongside him on the music. Trenet recorded it himself in 1946, on Columbia, the year after Gerbeau’s version and the year the song was published by Les Editions Raoul Breton. Jack Lawrence (1912-2009) supplied the English lyric that turned an ode to the sea into a love song.
