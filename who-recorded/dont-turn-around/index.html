Who recorded it first

Don't Turn Around

Not an Ace of Base original — a 1986 Tina Turner B‑side

Ace of Base’s 1994 smash is so completely theirs in most people’s memory that the song is assumed to have started in Gothenburg. It didn’t. “Don’t Turn Around” was written by Albert Hammond and Diane Warren and first recorded by Tina Turner, issued in August 1986 on Capitol as the B‑side of “Typical Male.” Even listeners who know it’s a cover usually stop at Aswad’s UK No. 1 from 1988 — but Aswad were third in line, after Turner and after Luther Ingram. Turner’s version was never worked as a single of its own, which is exactly why almost nobody heard it.

Fact-check

Right song, wrong starting point

Two separate mistakes stack up on this one — the common one, and the one made by people who think they’ve corrected it.

The belief1994 — the famous version

Ace of Base

Their single went to No. 4 in the US, No. 5 in the UK and No. 1 in Canada, and for most listeners it is simply what the song is. It is a cover, recorded in 1993 and released in March 1994.

The near miss1988 — UK No. 1, still a cover

Aswad

The usual correction is “actually it’s an Aswad song.” Their February 1988 reggae version did top the UK chart — but it came after Turner’s recording and after Luther Ingram’s 1987 version.

The originalAugust 1986 — Capitol B‑side

Tina Turner

Recorded during the Break Every Rule sessions and issued on the flip side of “Typical Male.” First on record, last in public memory.

The writersthe composition

Albert Hammond and Diane Warren

Neither performer wrote it. Hammond and Warren did, and Hammond later recorded it himself in 2010.

Written by Albert Hammond and Diane Warren; first recorded by Tina Turner, August 1986, as the B‑side of “Typical Male” on Capitol.

Context

Why the cover ate the original

A B‑side is a hiding place. “Don’t Turn Around” was recorded by one of the most famous singers on earth at the peak of her comeback, and still nobody heard it, because it was pressed onto the back of a single whose A‑side was doing all the work. Radio played “Typical Male.” Nothing pushed the flip.

What followed is unusual: the song was covered four separate times before Ace of Base got to it, and each cover found a different audience. Luther Ingram took it to the US R&B chart in 1987. Aswad turned it into a reggae record and took it to No. 1 in the UK in 1988. Bonnie Tyler and Neil Diamond each had a go. By 1994 the song had been in public circulation for eight years without anyone particularly associating it with Tina Turner — so when Ace of Base released the version that went to No. 4 in the US and No. 1 in Canada, there was no incumbent original in listeners’ memory for it to compete with. It simply moved in.

The chronology, step by step
  • Albert Hammond and Diane Warren write “Don’t Turn Around.”
  • August 1986 — Tina Turner records it during the Break Every Rule sessions, produced by Bob Clearmountain and Bryan Adams; Capitol issues it as the B‑side of “Typical Male.”
  • 1987 — Luther Ingram records it; his version reaches No. 55 on the US R&B chart.
  • February 1988 — Aswad release their reggae version on Mango/Island, from the album Distant Thunder; it goes to No. 1 in the UK.
  • 1988 — Bonnie Tyler records a version; Neil Diamond follows in 1991.
  • 1993 — Ace of Base record their version at Tuffs Studios, Gothenburg, with Tommy Ekman and Per Adebratt producing.
  • March 1994 — the Ace of Base single is released on Mega and Arista; it peaks at No. 4 in the US, No. 5 in the UK and No. 1 in Canada.
  • 2022 — Turner’s 1986 recording is remastered and included on the reissue of Break Every Rule, making the original easy to hear for the first time in decades.
Radio played the A‑side. The song was on the other one.
TT

The original recording artist

Tina Turner

1939-2023 · American rock and soul singer

By 1986 Turner was deep into the comeback that Private Dancer had launched, and Break Every Rule ↗ was the follow‑up built to keep it going. “Don’t Turn Around” came out of those sessions, produced by Bob Clearmountain and Bryan Adams, and was placed on the back of “Typical Male” — a single that went to No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100 while its flip side went essentially unnoticed. Turner recorded it first and then, in commercial terms, gave it away.

AB

Popularly credited as the original

Ace of Base

Swedish pop group, formed 1990 in Gothenburg

Ace of Base cut their version in 1993 at Tuffs Studios in Gothenburg with producers Tommy Ekman and Per Adebratt, and released it in March 1994 on Mega in Europe and Arista in the US. It reached No. 4 on the Billboard Hot 100, No. 5 in the UK and No. 1 in Canada, and became one of the defining radio records of that summer. Their reading is genuinely excellent — airy, unhurried, built on a reggae‑lite lilt that suits the lyric’s wounded calm. The only thing wrong with it is the date people attach to it.

HW

The songwriters

Albert Hammond & Diane Warren

British-Gibraltarian songwriter and American songwriter

Hammond and Warren wrote the song together, and it is one of the earliest of Warren’s enormous run of hits — a writer who would go on to be nominated for the Academy Award more times than almost anyone alive. Hammond, a hitmaker in his own right since the early 1970s, eventually recorded it himself for his 2010 album Legend. Because the composition is theirs rather than any performer’s, the song was free to travel: Luther Ingram, Aswad, Bonnie Tyler, Neil Diamond and Ace of Base all recorded it inside eight years.
