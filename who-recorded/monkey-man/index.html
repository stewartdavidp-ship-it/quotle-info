Who recorded it first

Monkey Man

Not a The Specials original — a 1969 Maytals single for Leslie Kong

Because it sits on The Specials’ 1979 debut and sounds like the purest thing 2 Tone ever made, “Monkey Man” is routinely taken for a Coventry original. It is not. The Maytals recorded it in 1969 at Dynamic Studios in Kingston for producer Leslie Kong, issued on his Beverley’s label in Jamaica and on Trojan in the UK, written by the group’s own Toots Hibbert. The original was no flop — it reached No. 47 on the UK singles chart and became the group’s first international hit — but the version most British listeners can hum arrived a full decade after the record it copies.

Fact-check

What people get wrong

“Monkey Man” is one of the most 2 Tone-sounding songs in the 2 Tone catalogue, which is precisely why it is so often filed as a Specials original. The recording history is not in dispute.

The beliefCover, 1979

The Specials

Their version appears on the self-titled debut released on 2 Tone on 19 October 1979, produced by Elvis Costello, an album that reached No. 4 in the UK. It is a cover, and the band described it as one.

The originalFirst recording, 1969

The Maytals

Recorded in 1969 at Dynamic Studios in Kingston for producer Leslie Kong, released as a single on Beverley’s in Jamaica and Trojan in the UK, backed with “Day and Night”. It reached No. 47 on the UK singles chart.

The writerWords and music

Frederick “Toots” Hibbert

Hibbert wrote the song and sang it as the Maytals’ frontman, so the original recording and the songwriting credit sit with the same act — there is no third party in the chain.

The producerContext, not credit

Leslie Kong

Kong produced the 1969 session and released it on his own Beverley’s label. He is central to why the record exists, but he did not write it and is not the recording artist.

The Specials covered it in 1979. The Maytals recorded it first, in 1969, and Toots Hibbert wrote it.

Context

Why the 2 Tone version swallowed the original

The Specials did not disguise where “Monkey Man” came from — the whole design of 2 Tone was to take Jamaican records made for a Jamaican audience and play them to a British one at punk volume. What that project could not control was memory. For most British listeners in 1979 the Maytals’ single was not a record they had grown up with; it was a No. 47 entry from spring 1970 that had come and gone. Hearing the song for the first time on a chart album that reached No. 4 made the cover feel like the source.

The other reason is the label. Leslie Kong’s Beverley’s was the origin, but Trojan was the imprint that carried it into Britain, and Trojan singles circulated as dance-floor currency rather than as authored works. A decade later 2 Tone gave those same songs sleeves, faces and an argument. Attribution follows packaging.

The chronology, step by step
  • 1962 — the Maytals form in Kingston as a vocal trio around Toots Hibbert.
  • 1967 — the group begins working with producer Leslie Kong, whose Beverley’s label issues their records.
  • 1969 — “Monkey Man”, written by Hibbert, is recorded at Dynamic Studios in Kingston and released as a single on Beverley’s in Jamaica, backed with “Day and Night”; Trojan issues it in the UK.
  • 1970 — the single enters the UK chart in April and peaks at No. 47, the group’s first international hit.
  • 1979 — The Specials record it for their Elvis Costello-produced debut on 2 Tone, released 19 October and peaking at No. 4 on the UK albums chart.
  • Later — further versions follow from General Levy (UK No. 75, 1993), Reel Big Fish (UK No. 86, 2003) and Amy Winehouse; Toots and the Maytals re-record it with No Doubt for the 2004 album True Love.
The Specials never hid the source. British memory just started the clock in 1979.
TM

The original recording artist

The Maytals

Jamaican vocal group formed in Kingston, 1962

Formed in Kingston in 1962 by Frederick “Toots” Hibbert with Henry “Raleigh” Gordon and Nathaniel “Jerry” Matthias, the Maytals began at Studio One and moved to producer Leslie Kong from 1967, a partnership that produced a run of hits across the late 1960s and early 1970s. “Monkey Man” was cut in 1969 at Dynamic Studios and released on Kong’s Beverley’s Records in Jamaica and on Trojan in Britain. It was the record that carried them beyond Jamaica for the first time.

TS

Popularly credited as the original

The Specials

British ska revival band formed in Coventry, 1977

Formed in Coventry in 1977, The Specials fused ska and rocksteady rhythms with punk energy and founded 2 Tone Records under Jerry Dammers in 1979. Their Elvis Costello-produced debut album, released on 19 October 1979, reached No. 4 in the UK and carried their reading of “Monkey Man”. Their version is superb and utterly their own — the only error in the popular account is chronological, and the band never pretended otherwise: covering Jamaican records was the whole point of 2 Tone.

TH

The songwriter

Toots Hibbert

1942–2020; Jamaican singer and songwriter, born in May Pen, Clarendon

Hibbert wrote both words and music of “Monkey Man” and sang the 1969 original as the Maytals’ frontman. Raised on gospel in May Pen, Clarendon, he moved to Kingston in the late 1950s and became one of the defining voices of ska and reggae — his 1968 single “Do the Reggay” is often credited with naming the genre. He died on 11 September 2020, aged 77.
