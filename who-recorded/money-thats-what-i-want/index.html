Who recorded it first

Money (That's What I Want)

Not a Beatles original — a 1959 Barrett Strong single on Tamla

Because John Lennon roars through it at the end of “With the Beatles,” the song is often filed under The Beatles. It isn’t theirs. Barrett Strong recorded it in Detroit and Tamla issued it as a single in August 1959 — the record that gave Berry Gordy’s young Motown operation its first real hit. The song was written by Berry Gordy and Janie Bradford; The Beatles taped their version on 18 July 1963, more than three years after Strong’s single went out.

How the record travelled

Recorded in Detroit in the summer of 1959, among the earliest sessions cut at Gordy’s West Grand Boulevard house — Benny Benjamin on drums, Eugene Grew on guitar, Brian Holland on tambourine, per Classic Motown ↗.

Tamla put the single out in August 1959; the record entered the Billboard Hot 100 in early 1960 and climbed to No. 2 R&B and No. 23 pop, giving Motown its first hit — see Wikipedia ↗.

The Beatles found Strong’s record at Brian Epstein’s NEMS shop, played it at their Decca audition on 1 January 1962, and cut it for EMI on 18 July 1963 — detail here ↗.

The Rolling Stones recorded it too: it appears on their debut EP, “The Rolling Stones” ↗, released by Decca on 10 January 1964.

Fact-check

Setting the record straight

The confusion here is about who cut it first, not about who wrote it. Performers rarely wrote their own material in this era, and that is not the story.

The beliefCover, 1963

The Beatles

Their scorching album closer on “With the Beatles” (22 November 1963) is the version most listeners encounter first, so the song reads as a Beatles original. It was recorded on 18 July 1963 and was never released as a single.

The originalFirst recording, 1959

Barrett Strong

Tamla issued his single (Tamla 54027) in August 1959, backed with “Oh I Apologize.” It reached No. 2 R&B and No. 23 on the Hot 100 in 1960 — Motown’s first hit.

The writersComposition

Berry Gordy and Janie Bradford

The credited composers. Strong disputed the credit for the rest of his life, saying he had helped write it; his name was on the original registration and was struck from it three years later.

Also recorded itCover, 1964

The Rolling Stones

Their debut EP, released by Decca on 10 January 1964, includes the song — further evidence of how thoroughly the British beat groups had absorbed Strong’s record.

Barrett Strong recorded it first, for Tamla, in August 1959. The Beatles were several acts down the line.

Context

Why the cover eclipsed the original

A hit record can still lose the memory war. Strong’s single sold and charted, but it went out on a label almost nobody had heard of, from a company operating out of a converted house on West Grand Boulevard, and had to be handed to Gwen Gordy’s Anna Records with Chess distribution just to reach shops nationwide. Four years later the same song went out on Parlophone attached to the biggest act on earth, on an album that sat at No. 1 in Britain for months.

There is also the matter of the performances. Strong’s is a swaggering, piano-driven R&B record, comfortable in its own confidence. Lennon’s is a man tearing his throat out. Both are terrific; only one of them was in every British household by Christmas 1963.

The chronology, step by step
  • Summer 1959 — recorded in Detroit at Gordy’s West Grand Boulevard studio, with Benny Benjamin on drums, Eugene Grew on guitar and Brian Holland on tambourine.
  • August 1959 — released as a single on Tamla 54027, backed with “Oh I Apologize.”
  • 1960 — leased to Anna Records (catalogue 1111) for national distribution through Chess; the record reaches No. 2 R&B and No. 23 on the Billboard Hot 100.
  • 1 January 1962 — The Beatles perform it at their unsuccessful Decca audition, with Pete Best on drums.
  • 18 July 1963 — The Beatles record it at EMI Studios in seven takes; George Martin overdubs piano.
  • 22 November 1963 — released as the closing track of “With the Beatles” on Parlophone.
  • 10 January 1964 — The Rolling Stones’ debut EP, carrying their own version, is released by Decca.
  • 10 April 1964 — the Beatles recording reaches America on “The Beatles’ Second Album.”
The record that paid for Motown is remembered as a Beatles track.
BS

The original recording artist

Barrett Strong

1941–2023 · American singer and songwriter · Detroit

Barrett Strong Jr. was born in West Point, Mississippi in 1941 and raised in Detroit. His 1959 Tamla single was the first hit record Berry Gordy’s operation ever had — the seed money, in every sense, for Motown. He never had another hit that size as a singer, but he became one of the label’s great lyricists, writing with producer Norman Whitfield for The Temptations, Marvin Gaye, Edwin Starr and Gladys Knight & the Pips. He was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2004 and died in January 2023.

TB

Popularly credited as the original

The Beatles

Formed Liverpool, 1960 · English rock band

The Beatles picked the record up at Brian Epstein’s NEMS shop and had been playing it live for years before they recorded it. Their version — taped at EMI Studios on 18 July 1963, John Lennon shredding his voice over George Martin’s piano — closes “With the Beatles” and is, by common consent, one of the fiercest things they ever put on tape. The only error in the popular memory is chronological: it is a cover, and they never pretended otherwise.

GB

The songwriters

Berry Gordy & Janie Bradford

Berry Gordy b. 1929; Janie Bradford b. 1939 · American songwriters, Detroit

Berry Gordy wrote the song’s hammering piano figure; Janie Bradford, then a teenager working at the fledgling label, worked the words out with him on the spot. Gordy would go on to build Motown into the most successful Black-owned business in America, and Bradford stayed with the company for decades. Barrett Strong long maintained he had contributed to the writing as well — his name appears on the original copyright registration but was removed three years later, which Gordy attributed to a clerical error.
