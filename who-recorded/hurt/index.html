Who recorded it first

Hurt

Not a Johnny Cash original — a 1994 Nine Inch Nails song

Johnny Cash’s 2002 recording is so devastating that a large audience — especially younger and country listeners — takes ‘Hurt’ for a Cash original. It is not. The song was written by Trent Reznor and first recorded by Nine Inch Nails in 1994, on the album The Downward Spiral. Cash covered it eight years later, near the end of his life.

How we traced it

‘Hurt’ was written by Trent Reznor and first recorded by his band Nine Inch Nails as the closing track of the 1994 album The Downward Spiral Wikipedia ↗.

Eight years later, producer Rick Rubin brought the song to Johnny Cash for American IV: The Man Comes Around (2002). Cash’s frail, valedictory reading — paired with Mark Romanek’s 2003 video — reframed the song entirely.

Reznor famously said that after seeing Cash’s version, ‘that song isn’t mine any more’ — a rare case of an original artist handing the song over. That surrender is a large part of why so many listeners now assume Cash wrote it the cover’s history ↗.

Fact-check

The attribution problem

This is a gentle kind of music misattribution: nothing about Cash’s record is wrong or fake, and Reznor himself blessed it. The only error is believing Cash originated it — a 2002 cover mistaken for the source.

Context

Why the cover ate the original

A cover eclipses its original most completely when it arrives with a story the original never had. Johnny Cash recorded ‘Hurt’ as a frail old man months from death, and Mark Romanek’s video read the song as an epitaph. Against that, a 1994 industrial-rock album track had little chance of staying the definitive version in the public mind.

The unusual twist is that the original artist agreed. Trent Reznor watched the Cash video and said the song no longer felt like his — so the eclipse was not a theft but a gift, which is exactly why it went unchallenged and why so many listeners now assume the song was Cash’s all along.

The song’s two lives, in order
  • Trent Reznor writes and records ‘Hurt’ for Nine Inch Nails, 1994; it closes The Downward Spiral.
  • The song is issued only as a promotional single, never a commercial one.
  • Rick Rubin brings it to Johnny Cash for American IV: The Man Comes Around, recorded 2002.
  • Mark Romanek’s video (2003) reframes the song as Cash’s farewell.
  • Cash dies in September 2003; his version becomes, for a large audience, the original.
The eclipse was not a theft but a gift — the writer himself said the song was no longer his.
NN

The original recording artist

Nine Inch Nails

formed 1988 · American industrial rock project of Trent Reznor

Nine Inch Nails is the industrial rock project founded by Trent Reznor in 1988. Reznor wrote and recorded ‘Hurt’ alone as the closing track of the 1994 album The Downward Spiral, and it stood for years as one of the band’s signature songs. That the original is now widely mistaken for a cover is a strange fate for a song this closely tied to its author.

JC

Popularly credited as the original

Johnny Cash

1932–2003 · American country and folk singer

Johnny Cash (1932–2003) recorded ‘Hurt’ in 2002, near the end of his life, for American IV: The Man Comes Around. His version is genuinely his and genuinely devastating; the only error is chronological, the assumption that a song sung by a dying legend must have started with him. It started in 1994, with Nine Inch Nails.

TR

The songwriter

Trent Reznor

b. 1965 · American musician, songwriter and producer

Trent Reznor (b. 1965) is the sole writer of ‘Hurt’ and the whole of Nine Inch Nails. After seeing Cash’s video he said the song no longer felt like his — a generous, unusual thing for a writer to say, and part of why his authorship has been so thoroughly eclipsed by the cover he admired.
