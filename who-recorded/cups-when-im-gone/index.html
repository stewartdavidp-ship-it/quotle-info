Who did it first

Cups (When I'm Gone)

Not an Anna Kendrick original — a 1931 folk song with a 2009 cup arrangement

This is a double misattribution. Almost everyone takes the cup song for an Anna Kendrick original, from Pitch Perfect (2012), but her version is neither the song’s origin nor the arrangement’s. The underlying song, ‘When I’m Gone’, is A.P. Carter’s, recorded by the Carter Family in 1931. The famous cup-tapping percussion arrangement everyone imitates was created by the British duo Lulu and the Lampshades and put on YouTube in 2009. Kendrick’s film performance came last, and it is what the public remembers as the source.

How we traced it

The song itself, ‘When I’m Gone’, was written by A.P. Carter and recorded by the Carter Family in 1931 — Carter was famous for collecting and reshaping Appalachian folk material, and the melody predates every version most people know Wikipedia ↗.

In 2009 the British duo Lulu and the Lampshades — Heloise Tunstall-Behrens and Luisa Gerstein — reworked Carter’s song and added the now-iconic cup-tapping percussion, uploading it to YouTube. That arrangement, not the 1931 recording, is what turned the song into a phenomenon Wikipedia ↗.

A viral cover by Anna Burden spread the cup game further, and Anna Kendrick discovered it that way before performing it in Pitch Perfect (2012). Her 2013 single reached the Billboard top 10 and so dominated public memory that most listeners assume she originated it — unaware of both the 1931 song and the 2009 arrangement Wikipedia ↗.

The attribution problem

This is a two-layer misattribution. Nothing about Anna Kendrick’s record is fake, but the public credits her with two things she did not do — writing the song and inventing the cup arrangement — when both belong to other people.

Context

Why the newest version ate the older two

Most cover-eclipses have one buried layer; this one has two. A 1931 folk song sat quietly in the Carter Family catalogue for decades, then in 2009 two British musicians reinvented it with a cup-percussion trick that made it irresistibly copyable. When Anna Kendrick performed that arrangement in a hit 2012 film, the movie’s reach simply overwrote everything beneath it.

The irony is that the cup game spread because it was easy to imitate — and every imitation carried the song further from its authors. By the time it reached the Billboard top 10, the folk writer and the arrangers had both vanished from the story, leaving only the most famous face attached to it.

The three layers, in order
  • A.P. Carter writes ‘When I’m Gone’; the Carter Family records it in 1931.
  • Lulu and the Lampshades add the cup-tapping arrangement and post it to YouTube, 2009.
  • Anna Burden’s cover goes viral after being shared on Reddit, 2011.
  • Anna Kendrick performs it in Pitch Perfect (2012), then releases it as a single (2013 → US No. 6).
  • The public takes Kendrick’s version as the origin, unaware of the 1931 song or the 2009 arrangement.
A 1931 folk song, a 2009 arrangement, a 2012 film — and the public remembers only the last one.
LL

The arrangement everyone imitates

Lulu and the Lampshades

British duo · formed 2009 (later Landshapes)

Lulu and the Lampshades were a British group founded in 2009 by Heloise Tunstall-Behrens and Luisa Gerstein, who later renamed themselves Landshapes. Their contribution is the part everyone actually copies: they took A.P. Carter’s old folk song and built the cup-tapping percussion around it, uploading the result to YouTube in 2009. Every hand-clapping, cup-sliding version that followed — including the one in Pitch Perfect — descends from theirs, though almost nobody knows their name.

AK

Popularly credited as the original

Anna Kendrick

b. 1985 · American actor and singer

Anna Kendrick (b. 1985) performed the cup song in Pitch Perfect (2012) and released it as a single in 2013, where it reached No. 6 on the Billboard Hot 100 — her first top-10 hit. She found the song through the same viral videos everyone else did, and never claimed to have written it; the misattribution is the audience’s, not hers. Her version is simply so famous that it buried the two layers of history beneath it.

AC

The songwriter

A.P. Carter

1891–1960 · American folk musician and song collector

A.P. Carter (1891–1960) was a founder of the Carter Family, one of the most influential acts in country music, and is credited with writing ‘When I’m Gone’, recorded in 1931. He was famous for roaming Appalachia collecting and reshaping folk songs, so the melody itself reaches back further still. He is the figure the cup game’s fame obscures most completely: not the viral performer, not the arranger, but the writer of the song underneath it all.
