Who recorded it first

Love Hurts

Not a Nazareth original — a 1960 Everly Brothers album track

Nazareth’s thunderous 1974 power ballad is so total a performance that the song reads as a Scottish hard-rock original. It isn’t. “Love Hurts” was written by Nashville songwriter Boudleaux Bryant and first recorded by The Everly Brothers in July 1960, then buried as track seven on their Warner Bros. album A Date with the Everly Brothers in October of that year. It was never issued as an Everly single — a dispute with their manager and publisher Wesley Rose kept it off the 45s — so the version that made the song famous in America was a cover recorded on the other side of the Atlantic.

How the song travelled

The original sits on the Everlys’ second Warner Bros. album, A Date with the Everly Brothers ↗, released in October 1960 — a record whose single, “Cathy’s Clown,” had already come out that April.

Roy Orbison took it to RCA Victor Studio B in Nashville on 27 February 1961 and put it out as the B-side of “Running Scared,” where it reached No. 5 in Australia — one of several stops charted on the song’s version history ↗.

Nazareth ↗, formed in Dunfermline in December 1968, released their version as a single on 8 November 1974; it became their only US Top Ten hit and the definitive reading for most listeners since.

Jim Capaldi’s version, released in November 1975, went to No. 4 in the UK — his highest-charting British single — and Cher recorded it twice, in 1975 and again in 1991.

Fact-check

Nazareth’s 1974 recording is the one nearly everybody has heard, and it is often taken to be where the song began. The chronology says otherwise.

Context

Why the cover swallowed the original

The interesting question is not why Nazareth’s version was big — it is why the Everlys’ version was invisible. It was never a single. It sat seventh on the second side of an album whose commercial job had already been done by “Cathy’s Clown,” and the Everlys’ relationship with their publisher, Wesley Rose of Acuff-Rose, soured badly enough that Rose cut them off from the Bryants’ catalogue between 1961 and 1964. A song with no 45, no promotion and a broken supply line behind it does not become anybody’s idea of the definitive recording.

Meanwhile the song kept travelling as a B-side and an album cut. Roy Orbison recorded it at RCA Victor Studio B in Nashville on 27 February 1961 and it went out as the flip of “Running Scared,” reaching No. 5 in Australia. Jim Capaldi’s reading, released in November 1975, hit No. 4 in the UK — his highest-charting British single. But only Nazareth’s took it into the American Top Ten, and in the pre-internet decades the hit single is what a song’s history collapses into.

The chronology, step by step
  • 30 August 1960 — Boudleaux Bryant’s composition is published by Acuff-Rose Publications.
  • July 1960 — The Everly Brothers record “Love Hurts” during the sessions for their second Warner Bros. album.
  • October 1960 — The recording is issued as an album track on A Date with the Everly Brothers (Warner Bros. W 1395). It is never released as a single.
  • 27 February 1961 — Roy Orbison records the song at RCA Victor Studio B, Nashville; it appears as the B-side of “Running Scared” and reaches No. 5 in Australia.
  • 8 November 1974 — Nazareth release their power-ballad version as a single (Mooncrest in the UK, A&M in the US and Canada).
  • April 1975 — Hair of the Dog is released; “Love Hurts” closes the international and US editions.
  • 1976 — Nazareth’s single reaches No. 8 on the Billboard Hot 100, the band’s only US Top Ten hit.
No single, no promotion — just track seven, waiting.

The original recording artist

The Everly Brothers

Don Everly 1937-2021 and Phil Everly 1939-2014 - American close-harmony duo

Don and Phil Everly recorded “Love Hurts” in July 1960 and released it that October on A Date with the Everly Brothers, their second album for Warner Bros. They had already reshaped popular music with “Bye Bye Love” and “Wake Up Little Susie,” and their close two-part harmony ran straight into The Beatles, The Beach Boys and Simon & Garfunkel. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in its inaugural 1986 class and the Country Music Hall of Fame in 2001.

NZ

Popularly credited as the original

Nazareth

Formed December 1968 in Dunfermline, Scotland - hard rock band

Nazareth cut “Love Hurts” as a slow-burning power ballad, Dan McCafferty’s sandpaper rasp doing the work the Everlys’ harmony had done. Released as a single on 8 November 1974 and later collected on 1975’s Hair of the Dog, it reached No. 8 on the US Billboard Hot 100 and went to No. 1 in Canada, the Netherlands, Belgium, Norway and South Africa. It remains the band’s only US Top Ten hit, and the only version of the song ever to be a US hit single. The record is genuinely great; the only error is chronological.

BB

The songwriter

Boudleaux Bryant

1920-1987 - American songwriter, Nashville

Boudleaux Bryant wrote “Love Hurts” alone; Acuff-Rose Publications registered it on 30 August 1960. With his wife Felice he formed one of the most successful songwriting partnerships in American music, credited with some 6,000 compositions and responsible for much of the Everly Brothers’ catalogue, including “Bye Bye Love,” “Wake Up Little Susie” and “All I Have to Do Is Dream,” as well as “Rocky Top.” The Bryants were inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 1986 and the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1991.
