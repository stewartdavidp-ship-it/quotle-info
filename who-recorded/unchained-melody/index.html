Who recorded it first

Unchained Melody

Not a Righteous Brothers original — Todd Duncan sang it first, for a 1955 prison film

The recording almost everyone meets first is The Righteous Brothers’ 1965 Philles single, and a great many people assume that is where the song begins. It is not. “Unchained Melody” was written by Alex North (music) and Hy Zaret (lyrics) as the theme for the Warner Bros. prison picture Unchained, and the first person to record it was the operatic baritone Todd Duncan, who cut it for that film’s soundtrack and also appeared on screen as a prisoner singing a shortened version. Duncan’s performance never became a hit record in its own right — within weeks the song was being carried up the charts by other singers entirely.

Context

Why the cover swallowed the original

The original had no chance to become famous, because it was never really a record — it was a piece of a film. Todd Duncan sang it into the soundtrack of Unchained ↗, a modest Warner Bros. prison drama that opened in January 1955, and performed an abbreviated version on screen as an inmate accompanied by another prisoner on guitar. That is a beautiful way to introduce a song and a hopeless way to sell one. The hits of 1955 belonged to people who had never been near the picture: Les Baxter’s orchestral treatment went to number one in the US, Al Hibbler reached number three, Roy Hamilton topped the R&B chart.

So the song was already a much-covered standard by the time Bill Medley and Bobby Hatfield got to it, and even they did not think they were making history. Phil Spector regarded “Unchained Melody” as album material and put it on the flip side of “Hung on You.” Disc jockeys turned the single over anyway, and it reached number four in the US. Then the 1990 film Ghost put that same 1965 recording in front of a generation with no memory of Les Baxter, let alone Todd Duncan — it re-entered the US chart at number thirteen and went to number one in the UK. A record made to fill space on a B-side became, for most listeners alive today, the song itself.

The chronology, step by step
  • Alex North composes the music as a theme for the prison film Unchained; Hy Zaret writes the lyric.
  • Todd Duncan records the vocal for the film’s soundtrack — his Wikipedia biography dates the session to 1954 — and appears in the picture as a prisoner singing a shortened version.
  • 19 January 1955: Unchained is released by Warner Bros. The song is nominated for the Academy Award for Best Song and loses to “Love Is a Many-Splendored Thing.”
  • 1955: commercial versions take over. Les Baxter reaches number one in the US, Al Hibbler number three (and number two in the UK), Roy Hamilton number one on the R&B chart and number six pop.
  • 2 March 1965: The Righteous Brothers record their version at Radio Recorders, Hollywood, with Bobby Hatfield singing lead alone.
  • 17 July 1965: Philles issues it as the B-side of “Hung on You.” Radio flips the record; it peaks at number four in the US and number fourteen in the UK.
  • 1990: Ghost revives the 1965 recording. It reaches number thirteen in the US and number one in the UK, and becomes the version most listeners assume is the original.
The first voice to sing it was a prisoner in a film nobody remembers.

The original recording artist

Todd Duncan

1903-1998 · American operatic baritone and actor

Robert Todd Duncan was born in Danville, Kentucky in 1903 and was George Gershwin’s personal choice to create the role of Porgy in the 1935 premiere of Porgy and Bess — a part he went on to play many hundreds of times. He taught voice at Howard University and, during the 1936 Washington run of Porgy and Bess, led the cast in a protest against the National Theatre’s segregated seating, refusing to perform in a house that barred him from buying certain seats. Film work was a sideline; he appeared in only two pictures, Syncopation and Unchained. It is the second of those that made him, almost incidentally, the first voice ever heard singing “Unchained Melody.”

RB

Popularly credited as the original

The Righteous Brothers

American duo · Bill Medley and Bobby Hatfield · recording issued 1965

Bill Medley and Bobby Hatfield recorded “Unchained Melody” at Radio Recorders in Hollywood on 2 March 1965, with Hatfield taking the lead entirely alone — one of the great solo vocals in pop, and the reason the record still stops rooms. Phil Spector treated it as filler; early pressings of the Philles single carried no producer credit for it at all, and by Medley’s account he handled the session himself. Hatfield reshaped the melody in the closing verse, and a great many of the covers recorded since follow his line rather than the one written for the film. Nothing about their claim on the song is dishonest — it is simply not the first recording.

AN

The songwriters

Alex North & Hy Zaret

Alex North 1910-1991, composer · Hy Zaret 1907-2007, lyricist · both American

Alex North wrote the music as a film theme. He was one of Hollywood’s most inventive scorers — A Streetcar Named Desire, Spartacus, Cleopatra — and collected fifteen Academy Award nominations without ever winning a competitive one, receiving an Honorary Oscar in 1986 instead. Hy Zaret was a Tin Pan Alley lyricist whose other credits ran from “One Meatball” to a series of educational science songs for children. Their song was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Song and lost to “Love Is a Many-Splendored Thing.” Zaret lived to 99, long enough to watch it become one of the most-recorded compositions of the twentieth century.
