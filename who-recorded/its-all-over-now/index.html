Who recorded it first

It's All Over Now

Not a Rolling Stones original — a 1964 Valentinos single on SAR Records

The Rolling Stones’ 1964 single is so completely the version people carry around that it is routinely assumed to be theirs. It is a cover. The song was written by Bobby Womack and his sister-in-law Shirley Womack, and first recorded by Womack’s group The Valentinos at United Recording in Hollywood on 24 March 1964, produced by Sam Cooke for his own SAR Records label. The Valentinos’ single crept onto the Billboard Hot 100 at No. 94 and was gone in two weeks; the Stones cut theirs at Chess Studios in Chicago on 10 June and released it on 26 June, and it became their first UK No. 1.

Who wrote it

Written by Bobby Womack — not The Rolling Stones

How we traced the writing

The song is credited to Bobby Womack and Shirley Womack; Bobby’s group The Valentinos recorded it first in 1964.

The Rolling Stones covered it that same year for their first UK No. 1, and it is so bound up with them that it is often taken for a Stones original. They made it a hit; Womack wrote it.

The same page’s other story is the recording (above): The Valentinos released it first, and the Stones’ cover eclipsed it.

Context

Why the cover swallowed the original

The interesting thing is not that the Stones covered it. It is how little daylight there was. The Valentinos’ single was still climbing when the Stones cut their version at Chess Studios, and both records were on the Billboard Hot 100 in the same summer. One entered at No. 94 and vanished in a fortnight; the other went to No. 1 in Britain. The original was not buried by time. It was overtaken almost in real time.

The mechanism was distribution and moment. SAR was Sam Cooke’s own small label, working an R&B market; the Stones had Decca, a British Invasion in full flood, and a DJ handing them the record. Bobby Womack’s own account — annoyance, then a royalty cheque, then complete forgiveness — is the neatest summary anyone has offered of what happened to his song.

The chronology, step by step
  • 24 March 1964 — The Valentinos record “It’s All Over Now” at United Recording in Hollywood, Sam Cooke producing, for SAR Records.
  • 1964 — SAR issues it as a single, catalogue number 152, backed with “Tired of Living in the Country.”
  • June 1964 — DJ Murray the K plays the record to The Rolling Stones in New York during their first North American tour.
  • 10 June 1964 — The Rolling Stones record their version at Chess Studios, Chicago.
  • 26 June 1964 — The Stones’ version is released on Decca F11934 in the UK; it becomes their first UK No. 1.
  • 27 June 1964 — The Valentinos’ single enters the Billboard Hot 100; it peaks at No. 94 and lasts two weeks.
Three months separated the record that made the song from the record that made it famous.

The original recording artist

The Valentinos

Active 1952–1974 · Cleveland, Ohio · SAR Records

Five Womack brothers — Friendly Jr., Curtis, Bobby, Harry and Cecil — who began singing in their father’s church around 1952 as the Womack Brothers. Sam Cooke signed them to SAR Records ↗ in 1960 and turned them secular as The Valentinos, scoring first with “Lookin’ for a Love.” Their “It’s All Over Now,” cut on 24 March 1964 with Bobby Womack on lead vocal and guitar and Cooke producing, is the first recording of the song. Cooke’s death that December, and the scandal that followed Bobby’s marriage to Cooke’s widow, effectively ended the group’s run.

RS

Popularly credited as the original

The Rolling Stones

Formed 1962, London · Decca (UK) / London (US)

They heard the Valentinos’ record in New York in June 1964, played to them by DJ Murray the K during their first North American tour, and recorded their own version at Chess Studios in Chicago days later. Released on 26 June 1964 on Decca F11934, it gave them their first UK No. 1 and reached No. 26 on the Billboard Hot 100. The performance — Keith Richards’ lead against Brian Jones’ twelve-string, with Ian Stewart on piano — is a genuinely great record. The only error in the popular account is chronological.

BW

The songwriters

Bobby Womack and Shirley Womack

Bobby Womack, 1944–2014 · co-written with his sister-in-law Shirley Womack

Bobby Womack wrote the song with Shirley Womack, his sister-in-law, and sang it himself on the Valentinos’ record. He objected when the Stones wanted it — the story, as Wikipedia and other accounts tell it, is that he wanted Mick Jagger to go and find his own song — and Sam Cooke talked him round. When the first royalty cheque arrived he reversed himself entirely and said Jagger could have anything he wanted. Womack went on to a long solo career as one of soul’s most durable writers and guitarists.
