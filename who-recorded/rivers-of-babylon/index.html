Who recorded it first

Rivers of Babylon

Not a Boney M. original — The Melodians recorded it in Jamaica in 1970

Boney M.’s 1978 disco version is one of the best-selling singles in British history, and most listeners file the song as a Euro-disco creation. It is a cover. “Rivers of Babylon” was written by Brent Dowe and Trevor McNaughton of the Jamaican group The Melodians and recorded by them in 1970 for producer Leslie Kong’s Beverley’s label. The Jamaican government initially banned it as subversive because of its Rastafarian framing; once the ban was lifted it took three weeks to reach number one in Jamaica, and the soundtrack to the 1972 film The Harder They Come carried it to the rest of the world.

How we know

The song was written and recorded in 1970 by Brent Dowe and Trevor McNaughton of The Melodians, with lyrics adapted from Psalms 137 and 19 — see Wikipedia’s article on the song ↗.

The Jamaican government initially banned the record for its Rastafarian references; once lifted, it took three weeks to top the Jamaican chart, per the same Wikipedia article ↗.

Context

Why the cover swallowed the original

The Melodians’ record was never obscure — it was a Jamaican number one and it opened a globally distributed film soundtrack. What it lacked was European radio. Boney M. arrived on a continental pop machine built by Frank Farian, with a Hansa/Atlantic release, a television-ready group and an arrangement engineered for the 1978 discotheque. That record sold in numbers that simply rewrite public memory: over two million UK copies, one of only seven singles ever to pass two million there and one of the ten best-selling British singles ever pressed.

There is a second reason the authorship blurred. The first pressings of the Boney M. single credited only Frank Farian and Reyam (Hans-Jörg Mayer), so the label copy that reached the earliest European buyers carried German names alone; Dowe and McNaughton were added on later pressings, after an agreement. Combined with a sound that gave no audible clue of rocksteady origin, the song arrived in Europe looking like something that had been made there.

The chronology, step by step
  • 1963 — The Melodians form in Greenwich Town, Kingston: Tony Brevett, Brent Dowe, Trevor McNaughton.
  • 1970 — Dowe and McNaughton write “Rivers of Babylon”, adapting Psalms 137 and 19. The Melodians record it for Leslie Kong at Beverley’s.
  • 1970 — The Jamaican government bans the record as subversive; after the ban is lifted it reaches number one in Jamaica within three weeks. It is also the title track of the group’s Beverley’s album, catalogue BLP 019.
  • 1971 — Leslie Kong dies; the group moves to other producers.
  • 1972 — The Melodians’ recording appears on the Island soundtrack album to the film The Harder They Come, reaching an international audience.
  • 3 April 1978 — Boney M. release their version on Hansa, Sire and Atlantic, produced by Frank Farian. The first pressings credit only Farian and Reyam (Hans-Jörg Mayer); Dowe and McNaughton are added on later pressings after an agreement. It tops charts across Europe and passes two million UK copies.

The original recording artist

The Melodians

Jamaican vocal group, formed Kingston 1963 · Brent Dowe, Trevor McNaughton, Tony Brevett

The Melodians were formed in the Greenwich Town area of Kingston in 1963 by Tony Brevett, Brent Dowe and Trevor McNaughton, and became one of the great harmony groups of the rocksteady era. Their biggest record was made for producer Leslie Kong at Beverley’s, and “Rivers of Babylon” became an anthem of the Rastafari movement. Its inclusion on the soundtrack of The Harder They Come ↗ in 1972 turned a Jamaican hit into an international one. Kong died in 1971, and the group afterwards recorded for other producers including Lee Perry and Byron Lee’s Dynamic Studios.

BM

Popularly credited as the original

Boney M.

West German vocal group, formed 1974 by producer Frank Farian

Boney M. were assembled in West Germany in 1974 by producer Frank Farian, fronted by Liz Mitchell, Marcia Barrett, Maizie Williams and Bobby Farrell. Their “Rivers of Babylon” was issued on 3 April 1978 and went to number one across most of Europe, selling over two million copies in the UK alone — one of only seven singles ever to pass two million there — and becoming one of that country’s all-time best-selling singles. The record is a genuinely inspired reworking — the only error is chronological. The first single pressings credited only Frank Farian and Reyam (Hans-Jörg Mayer); Dowe and McNaughton were added to the credit on later pressings, after an agreement.

BD

The songwriters

Brent Dowe and Trevor McNaughton

Jamaican singers and songwriters; Dowe died 2006, McNaughton died 2018

Dowe and McNaughton wrote “Rivers of Babylon” as members of The Melodians, adapting the texts of Psalms 137 and 19 into a Rastafari song. McNaughton had the idea of forming the group and brought in Tony Brevett, who recruited Brent Dowe; Dowe shared lead vocals with Brevett and left for a solo career in 1973. Dowe died of a heart attack in January 2006 at the age of 59. McNaughton, the last surviving original member, died on 20 November 2018 at 77.
