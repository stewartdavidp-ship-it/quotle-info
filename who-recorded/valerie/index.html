Who recorded it first

Valerie

Not an Amy Winehouse original — a 2006 Zutons song

Almost everyone hears ‘Valerie’ as an Amy Winehouse song, but the version she features on is a cover. The song was written and first recorded by The Zutons, a Liverpool band, in 2006 — a UK No. 9 hit. Mark Ronson covered it a year later with Winehouse on vocals, and that 2007 version so eclipsed the original that the song is now remembered as definitively hers.

How we traced it

‘Valerie’ was written and first recorded by The Zutons, a Liverpool band, and released as a single in 2006 from their second album Tired of Hanging Around. The songwriting is credited to all five members, with frontman Dave McCabe as principal writer Wikipedia ↗.

The original was a real hit — No. 9 on the UK Singles Chart — but a distinctly indie-rock record, and outside Britain it was little known.

Mark Ronson recorded a brass-driven soul arrangement for his 2007 covers album Version, with Amy Winehouse on lead vocals. Released that October, it reached No. 2 in the UK and became so dominant that most listeners now take it for one of Winehouse’s own songs Wikipedia ↗.

Fact-check

The attribution problem

This is a gentle kind of misattribution: the famous version really is a Mark Ronson and Amy Winehouse record, and a great one. The errors are believing Winehouse originated the song — and that she recorded it alone — when it is a 2007 cover of a 2006 Zutons single.

Context

Why the cover ate the original

A cover eclipses its original most easily when the two live in different worlds. The Zutons’ ‘Valerie’ was a British indie-rock hit; Mark Ronson’s 2007 version rebuilt it as brass-heavy retro soul and handed it to a once-in-a-generation voice. It was not merely more popular — it reached a far wider audience, and the earlier record receded behind it.

The irony is that Amy Winehouse never claimed the song, and the credit never hid Mark Ronson — yet after her death the track folded into her legacy anyway. When a cover becomes the emotional centre of a performer’s story, listeners stop hearing it as anyone else’s song, and the band who wrote it slips out of the frame.

The two records, in order
  • The Zutons write ‘Valerie’ and record it first (2006, Deltasonic).
  • The original reaches No. 9 on the UK Singles Chart — a hit, but indie-rock and mostly British.
  • Mark Ronson cuts a soul arrangement for his covers album Version, with Amy Winehouse on vocals.
  • Released October 2007 → UK No. 2, one of the year’s biggest-selling singles.
  • After Winehouse’s death in 2011, the cover becomes the definitive version, and the original fades from public memory.
Winehouse never claimed the song — her voice claimed it for her.
TZ

The writers and original recording artist

The Zutons

Liverpool rock band · formed 2001

The Zutons were a Liverpool rock band who both wrote ‘Valerie’ and recorded it first, releasing it as a single in 2006 from their album Tired of Hanging Around. The song is credited to all five members, with frontman Dave McCabe as its principal writer. Their original reached No. 9 in the UK — a genuine hit — yet within a year it was almost entirely overshadowed by a cover, and their authorship is the part of the story most listeners never learn.

AW

Popularly credited as the original

Amy Winehouse

1983–2011 · English soul and jazz singer

Amy Winehouse (1983–2011) sang lead on Mark Ronson’s 2007 cover of ‘Valerie’, and her voice so completely redefined the song that it is now hers in the public mind. The record is properly credited to Ronson featuring Winehouse, and it is a cover of The Zutons — but after her death in 2011 it settled into memory as one of her signature recordings, helped along by its place in the 2015 documentary Amy. The performance is the reason for the confusion; the only error is forgetting it was a cover at all.
