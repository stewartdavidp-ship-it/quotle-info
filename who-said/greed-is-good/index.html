Who really said it

Greed is good

Not quite — the actual line is “greed, for lack of a better word, is good” Stanley Weiser & Oliver Stone Credited screenwriters · Wall Street (1987)
? Attribution status: Disputed ©Reuse status: In copyright

In Wall Street (1987), written by Stanley Weiser and Oliver Stone, Gordon Gekko tells the Teldar Paper shareholders that “greed, for lack of a better word, is good” — a hedge that the popular four-word version deletes. The compression also hands the film a phrase it did not coin: “Greed is good” was already in print before the movie opened.

Fact-check

Who gets credited, and why it's wrong

Nobody is falsely credited here in the usual sense — the film is real, the speech is real, and the writers are correctly named. What went wrong is the wording, and then a second thing went wrong on top of it: the film got credit for coining a phrase that was already circulating.

✕
the wordingGordon Gekko / Michael Douglas never said it

The four words appear nowhere in the film. The transcript reads: “The point is, ladies and gentlemen, that greed, for lack of a better word, is good. Greed is right, greed works.” Even the American Film Institute, ranking it #57 on its 2005 list, printed the hedged version ↗. Douglas is also the actor, not the author — the words are Weiser and Stone's.

✕
the real person behind GekkoIvan Boesky said something else

The arbitrageur told UC Berkeley's business graduates on 18 May 1986: “Greed is all right, by the way… I think greed is healthy. You can be greedy and still feel good about yourself.” Stone credits that speech as the seed of Gekko's line in his DVD commentary. But Boesky never said “greed is good” — though he was already being quoted as saying it by May 1987 ↗, months before the film opened.

✕
coinageWall Street (1987) itself didn't coin it

The exact phrase was in print well before the screenplay. Barry Popik documents Mother Jones using it in October 1984 ↗ — “It's O.K. to grab. Greed is good. Rich is respectable” — and Michael Korda heading a chapter of Success! (1977) “Greed Is Good for You.” The film made the phrase famous; it did not invent it.

✕
the sequelGordon Gekko, again misquotes himself

By Wall Street: Money Never Sleeps (2010), written by Allan Loeb and Stephen Schiff, the character has adopted the public's misquote as his own memory: “Someone reminded me I once said ‘Greed is good’. Now it seems it's legal.” ↗ He didn't.

✓ The honest summary is Barry Popik's: “Greed is good” became popular from Boesky's 1986 speech and the 1987 film — but neither of them used those exact words ↗. Weiser and Stone wrote “greed, for lack of a better word, is good”; the culture kept the slogan and threw away the hesitation.

Context

A hedge that got edited out

Gordon Gekko is not being glib when he reaches for the word. He is a corporate raider addressing a shareholders' meeting, and he is trying to relabel something — to sell asset-stripping as evolution. “For lack of a better word” is the sound of a man reaching for a euphemism, failing to find one, and brazening it out. Cut those five words and you remove the strain: what is left is a slogan, delivered without hesitation, that a certain kind of viewer could hang on a wall.

That is the misquote's real damage. The film convicts Gekko — he goes to jail — but the compressed line escaped the movie and became a creed, which is why Stanley Weiser eventually wrote a newspaper column titled “Repeat After Me: Greed is Not Good.” The flattening began almost immediately: Time's review, published three days after the film opened, already had Gekko proclaiming “greed is good, greed is right, greed works.”

“For lack of a better word” is the whole performance. Delete it and Gekko stops hesitating.

The phrase was loose in the culture before the film

Michael Korda titled a chapter of his 1977 bestseller Success! “Greed Is Good for You.” Mother Jones ran the bare four words in October 1984, on Reagan: “It's O.K. to grab. Greed is good. Rich is respectable.” Both predate the screenplay.

The misquote was attached to Ivan Boesky before Wall Street ever reached cinemas. In February 1987 Joe Biden was attacking Reagan for leading a “greed is good” decade, and by May 1987 — seven months before release — he was telling the Washington Post that Boesky “would be applauded by a California graduating class for saying ‘Greed is good.’” Boesky had not said it either.

In the 2010 sequel Wall Street: Money Never Sleeps (written by Allan Loeb and Stephen Schiff), Gekko misquotes himself: “Someone reminded me I once said ‘Greed is good’. Now it seems it's legal.” The character has absorbed the public's version of his own speech.

About Stanley Weiser & Oliver Stone

SW

Stanley Weiser & Oliver Stone

Credited screenwriters · Wall Street (1987), 20th Century Fox

Stanley Weiser and Oliver Stone share the writing credit on Wall Street (1987), which Stone also directed. Gordon Gekko was assembled from several real 1980s financiers — Ivan Boesky, Carl Icahn, Dennis Levine, Asher Edelman and others — rather than drawn from one man. Both writers have spent decades pointing out that the film's most-quoted sentiment belongs to its villain, who goes to prison in the last act; in 2008 Weiser published a Los Angeles Times op-ed under the title “Repeat After Me: Greed is Not Good.”
