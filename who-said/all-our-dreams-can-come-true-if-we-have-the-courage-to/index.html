Who really said it

All our dreams can come true, if we have the courage to pursue them

Not Walt Disney — unverified & likely apocryphal No verified author (falsely credited to Walt Disney) Earliest known print: 1992 — 26 years after Disney’s death
? Attribution status: Disputed

This is a classic feel-good line pinned on Walt Disney with no primary source behind it. Disney Archives staff and historians (Marty Sklar, Jim Korkis, Jeff Kurti among them) report they “can’t find verification of that ANYWHERE,” and the earliest documented appearance is in The Goal Book (1992), p. vi — most likely derived from the Disneyland tagline “Where Dreams Come True” and “probably apocryphal.” Disney died in 1966; there is no record of him saying or writing this.

How we verified this

  • Disney historian Barry Linetsky files this under fake quotes, quoting a Disney Family Museum researcher: “We can’t find verification of that ANYWHERE … it doesn’t appear in publication” — after the Archives, WDI, Marty Sklar, Jim Korkis and Jeff Kurti all searched.
  • LibQuotes traces the earliest print to The Goal Book (1992), p. vi, and judges it “most likely derived from the tag line ‘Disneyland: Where Dreams Can Come True’ … probably apocryphal.”
  • On Wikiquote the only citation offered is Pat Williams’ 2004 biography How to Be Like Walt (p. 63) — a secondary compilation published decades after Disney’s death, not anything Disney himself said or wrote.

The exact wording has no identifiable author: its earliest located appearance is an anonymous 1992 goal-setting booklet, and no primary Disney source exists. Because the origin is unknown, no copyright or public-domain status can honestly be asserted.

Context

Why it sticks to Disney

Walt Disney’s entire brand is dreams-made-real, so a tidy sentence about dreams and courage feels unmistakably ‘his’ — which is exactly why it attaches to him so easily and resists correction. The line reads like distilled Disney marketing, and LibQuotes suggests it may in fact be a paraphrase of the Disneyland tagline “Where Dreams Come True” rather than a personal remark.

The same dynamic produced “If you can dream it, you can do it,” another perennial ‘Disney’ quote actually written by Imagineer Tom Fitzgerald years after Walt’s death. In both cases the sentiment is on-brand; the sourcing is not.

It’s very ‘Wisdom-ish’ — but it doesn’t appear in publication.

About the man it’s pinned on

Walt Disney

WD

Walt Disney

1901–1966 · American animator, film producer & co-founder of The Walt Disney Company

Walt Disney (1901–1966) was the American animator and entrepreneur behind Mickey Mouse, Snow White and Disneyland. Because his brand is built on dreams and imagination, uplifting “dream” lines are constantly manufactured and pinned to him — this quotation and the equally misattributed “If you can dream it, you can do it” being the two most common. His genuinely documented remarks tend to stress hard work and courage as action rather than aphorism.
