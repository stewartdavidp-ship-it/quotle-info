Who really said it

If you don't like something, change it. If you can't change it, change your attitude

Attributed to Maya Angelou 1928–2014 · American memoirist, poet & civil-rights voice
≈ Attribution status: Attributed

Widely credited to Maya Angelou and genuinely rooted in her writing: her 1993 essay collection ‘Wouldn’t Take Nothing for My Journey Now’ contains “What you’re supposed to do when you don’t like a thing is change it. If you can’t change it, change the way you think about it.” The crisp popular wording — with ‘change your attitude’ — is a paraphrase, not a sentence found verbatim in any primary source.

Fact-check

The wording problem

This is not a case of the wrong author — the thought is authentically Angelou’s. The catch is the wording: the polished poster version is a paraphrase that drifted from what she actually wrote.

✕
Circulated as her exact words: “…change your attitude” paraphrase

Angelou’s 1993 book reads “change the way you think about it,” not “change your attitude,” and the compressed one-liner appears in quote databases with no source cited — see the unsourced community entry on Goodreads ↗ versus the sourced book wording here ↗.

✓ Bottom line: the sentiment is genuinely Maya Angelou’s, from ‘Wouldn’t Take Nothing for My Journey Now’ (1993) ↗ — but the crisp “change your attitude” form is a paraphrase, not her verbatim line.

Context

Why it resonates

In Wouldn’t Take Nothing for My Journey Now (1993), Angelou frames the line as hard-won counsel against the corrosive habit of complaint: “So you watch yourself about complaining, Sister. What you’re supposed to do when you don’t like a thing is change it. If you can’t change it, change the way you think about it. Don’t complain.”

Stripped to a two-clause maxim and given the punchier “change your attitude,” it became one of Angelou’s most-shared motivational lines — which is exactly how the paraphrase hardened into ‘the quote.’

Change it — or change the way you think about it. Don’t complain.

On the ‘attributed’ call

Because a real, published Angelou passage plainly underlies the line, this is not a misattribution to the wrong person — it is genuinely hers in substance. It is filed as attributed rather than verified only because the specific words people quote (“change your attitude”) are a paraphrase that does not appear verbatim in any primary source.

About Maya Angelou

MA

Maya Angelou

1928–2014 · American memoirist, poet & civil-rights activist

Maya Angelou (born Marguerite Annie Johnson, 1928–2014) was an American memoirist, poet, and civil-rights activist, best known for her landmark 1969 autobiography I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings. Her 1993 essay collection Wouldn’t Take Nothing for My Journey Now gathered aphoristic reflections on courage, faith, and living well — among them the counsel against complaining that this quote paraphrases. Angelou’s fame has also made her one of the most misquoted authors online, which makes separating her real words from smoothed paraphrases worthwhile.
