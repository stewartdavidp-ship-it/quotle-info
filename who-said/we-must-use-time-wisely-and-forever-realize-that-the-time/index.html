Who really said it

We must use time wisely and forever realize that the time is always ripe to do right

Not Nelson Mandela — actually Martin Luther King Jr. Martin Luther King Jr. 1929–1968 · Baptist minister and civil-rights leader; written 16 April 1963
? Attribution status: Disputed

The line is a slightly reworded version of a sentence from Martin Luther King Jr.’s “Letter from Birmingham Jail” (16 April 1963), whose original text reads “We must use time creatively, in the knowledge that the time is always ripe to do right.” It is not from Nelson Mandela; the Mandela credit is a modern misattribution of a garbled King quotation.

Original wording
“…creatively, in the knowledge that…”

Human progress never rolls in on wheels of inevitability; it comes through the tireless efforts of men willing to be co-workers with God, and without this hard work, time itself becomes an ally of the forces of social stagnation. We must use time creatively, in the knowledge that the time is always ripe to do right. Now is the time to make real the promise of democracy… ↑ King wrote “creatively, in the knowledge that” — the Mandela version swaps in “wisely and forever realize”

King’s point is that time is neutral: it can serve justice or stagnation, so people must act rather than wait. The circulated Mandela quotation lifts only the second sentence and softens its wording.

Fact-check

The misattribution

The quotation circulates widely under Nelson Mandela’s name, usually in the form “We must use time wisely and forever realize that the time is always ripe to do right.” That is a reworded fragment of a King sentence, and no primary Mandela source exists for it.

✕
Incorrectly attributed to Nelson Mandela

Quote sites such as Goodreads ↗ and Quotefancy list “We must use time creatively, and forever realize that the time is always ripe to do right” as Mandela’s, but none cite a speech, book, or letter — and the wording matches King’s 1963 sentence, not any documented Mandela text.

✓ The words originate with Martin Luther King Jr. in the Letter from Birmingham Jail ↗ (16 April 1963): “We must use time creatively, in the knowledge that the time is always ripe to do right.”

Context

Why it mattered

King wrote the letter in the margins of a newspaper and on scraps of paper after his arrest during the 1963 Birmingham desegregation campaign. Eight local clergymen had published a statement calling the protests “unwise and untimely” and urging Black residents to wait.

The sentence about time is King’s answer: progress is not automatic, and “the time is always ripe to do right” means there is never a wrong moment to act justly. Stripped of that context and re-credited to Mandela, the line loses its specific argument against the politics of ‘wait.’

The true author

About the real source

MLK

Martin Luther King Jr.

1929–1968 · minister and civil-rights leader

Martin Luther King Jr. (1929–1968) was the Baptist minister who led the American civil-rights movement and won the 1964 Nobel Peace Prize. He wrote the “Letter from Birmingham Jail” on 16 April 1963 while jailed for nonviolent protest, answering white clergymen who called his campaign “untimely.” The line about time being “always ripe to do right” is his direct rebuttal to that call for patience.
