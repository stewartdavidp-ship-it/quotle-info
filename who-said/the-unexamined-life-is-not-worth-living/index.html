Who really said it

The unexamined life is not worth living

Spoken by Socrates c. 470–399 BC · Athenian philosopher, as recorded by Plato
✓ Attribution status: Verified

The line is from Socrates’ defense speech at his trial in 399 BC, preserved in Plato’s Apology at section 38a. It is one of the best-documented quotations in the Western canon — with the important caveat that Socrates wrote nothing himself, so we have it only through his pupil Plato’s account.

Fact-check

Correctly Socrates — but watch the fine print

This is a rare case where the popular attribution is right. The confusions are subtler than a wrong name:

✕
Sometimes credited to Plato understandable

Because the line only exists in Plato’s Apology ↗, it is often filed under Plato. Within the text, though, the words are spoken by Socrates in his own defense — Plato is the author-narrator, not the speaker.

✕
Frequently stripped of its meaning

Self-help and motivational culture rebrand the line as a call to “find yourself.” As The Collector notes ↗, Socrates meant rigorous public philosophical inquiry into virtue, not private introspection or career planning.

✓ Bottom line: genuinely Socrates, spoken at his trial and recorded in Plato’s Apology 38a ↗ — just remember it reaches us only through Plato’s pen.

Context

Why he said it

Socrates spoke these words near the end of his trial in 399 BC, after the jury had found him guilty. Offered the chance to propose exile or to simply stop philosophizing as an alternative to death, he refused: to give up the daily examination of virtue, he argued, would empty life of its worth.

The famous clause is the culmination of that refusal — a defense of the philosophical life so uncompromising that he preferred execution to abandoning it.

The unexamined life is not worth living for men.

The Socratic problem

Because Socrates left no writings, historians distinguish the “historical Socrates” from the character who appears in Plato’s dialogues. Whether the man actually uttered this exact sentence at his trial, or whether Plato crafted it as the perfect distillation of his teacher’s creed, cannot be settled — a limitation scholars call the “Socratic problem.”

What is certain is that the line is Plato’s record of Socrates, composed within a generation of the trial, and has been read as Socrates’ own for nearly 2,400 years.

About Socrates

S

Socrates

c. 470–399 BC · Athenian philosopher

Socrates was a classical Athenian philosopher widely regarded as a founder of Western moral philosophy. Famous for the “Socratic method” of relentless questioning, he wrote nothing himself — almost everything we know of him comes through his pupils Plato and Xenophon. Tried on charges of impiety and corrupting the youth, he was sentenced to death and executed by hemlock in 399 BC.
