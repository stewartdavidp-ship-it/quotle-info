Fact-check

What travels with the quote

The attribution to Marie Curie is correct — but the wording and the sourcing that usually accompany it are not.

Context

Where it comes from

Marie Curie was famously reluctant to talk about herself. She wrote her brief Autobiographical Notes in 1923 only at the insistence of the American journalist Marie Mattingly Meloney, to accompany a memoir of her husband Pierre. The line about progress comes from that short memoir — recalling not her Nobel-winning discoveries but her earliest, fumbling experiments as a young woman.

In the passage she is describing teaching herself physics and chemistry in a small municipal laboratory in Warsaw, run by one of her cousins, with time to work only in the evenings and on Sundays — “encouraged by a little unhoped-for success” one day and “in the deepest despair” the next. The famous maxim is really her wry summary of that apprenticeship.

though I was taught that the way of progress is neither swift nor easy…

The subtle drift from “is” to “was” changes the tone slightly: the present tense frames it as a standing lesson she carried forward, not a closed chapter. Quote sites almost universally print the past-tense version, stripped of the surrounding sentence about her first trial at experimental research.

About Marie Curie

MC

Marie Curie

1867–1934 · physicist and chemist, two-time Nobel laureate

Born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, Marie Curie was a Polish-French physicist and chemist who pioneered research on radioactivity — a term she and Pierre coined. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize and remains the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two sciences: Physics (1903) and Chemistry (1911). She wrote her brief autobiographical notes in 1923 at the urging of the American journalist Marie Mattingly Meloney, appending them to her memoir of her late husband Pierre.
