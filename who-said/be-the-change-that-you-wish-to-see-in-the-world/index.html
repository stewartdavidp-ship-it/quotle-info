Who really said it

Be the change that you wish to see in the world

Not Gandhi — first written by Arleen Lorrance American educator · 1974
? Attribution status: Disputed

Universally credited to Mahatma Gandhi, but the exact phrase traces to educator Arleen Lorrance (1974). Gandhi expressed a kindred idea in 1913 — in very different words.

One way to start a preventative program is to be the change you want to see happen. That is the essence and substance of The Love Project. ↑ the tidy “…in the world” slogan came later

Gandhi did write a kindred idea in 1913 — “If we could change ourselves, the tendencies in the world would also change” — which is why the slogan feels like his. But those are different words, and the compact sentence isn't in his Collected Works.

How we verified this

  • Earliest documented wording traced to Arleen Lorrance's 1974 contribution to the anthology Developing Priorities and a Style — documented in full by Quote Investigator.
  • The exact seven-word slogan is absent from Gandhi's roughly 100-volume Collected Works, searchable via the Gandhi Heritage Portal; the M. K. Gandhi Institute has acknowledged the line is not found in his writings.
  • The kindred authentic passage — “We but mirror the world…” — is confirmed in Gandhi's Collected Works, Vol. 12, p. 158 (Indian Opinion, 1913).
  • Independent fact-checks concur — Check Your Fact rates the Gandhi attribution unsupported.

Usage & rights: A short phrase like this generally isn't protected by copyright — but attribution is the point here. Credit the idea's modern origin to Arleen Lorrance (1974), and the kindred 1913 passage to Gandhi. Gandhi's 1913 text is public domain; the seven-word slogan should not be credited to him.

Why everyone says Gandhi

This is one of the most-shared “Gandhi” quotes on the internet. Here's why it isn't his — and where it really came from:

✕
Popularly attributed to Mahatma Gandhi

The phrase drifted onto Gandhi in the 1980s and then exploded across merchandise and social media. But it appears nowhere in his Collected Works, and no primary source connects it to him. Traced by Quote Investigator ↗

✕
Sometimes passed off as an ancient proverb, or “anonymous” rare

Context

Why it mattered

The genuine idea is deeply Gandhian. In 1913 — while leading nonviolent resistance in South Africa — Gandhi wrote that outer change begins with inner change: “As a man changes his own nature, so does the attitude of the world change towards him.” That conviction sits at the heart of his philosophy of self-rule.

The idea is Gandhi's. The seven words are not.

Read the full story

Stripped to a slogan, the Lorrance-derived line became a globally portable call to personal responsibility — precisely because it distills (even if it didn't originate from) Gandhi's actual teaching. That resonance is exactly why the misattribution stuck so hard.

It's now a textbook case for editors and fact-checkers of how a paraphrase migrates into a famous name — the very problem a verified-provenance page exists to fix.

The real author

About Arleen Lorrance

AL

Arleen Lorrance

American educator & writer · co-founder of The Love Project

Arleen Lorrance is an American educator, writer, and co-founder of The Love Project. She coined the modern “be the change you want to see happen” phrasing in 1974, in the context of her work at Thomas Jefferson High School in Brooklyn — the earliest documented appearance of the line later mis-credited to Gandhi.
