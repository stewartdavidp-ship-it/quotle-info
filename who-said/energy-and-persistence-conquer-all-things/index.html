Energy and persistence conquer all things.

No Franklin document is known to contain these words. The nearest authentic lines are his own Poor Richard maxims: “Diligence overcomes Difficulties, Sloth makes them” (1755) and “All things are easy to Industry, all things difficult to Sloth” (1734).

How we checked it

  • A full-text search of the Papers of Benjamin Franklin ↗ returns no document containing “energy and persistence conquer all things”; the phrase is likewise absent from Wikiquote’s Franklin page ↗, sourced and misattributed sections alike.
  • Franklin’s authentic Poor Richard sayings carry the identical idea — e.g. “Diligence overcomes Difficulties, Sloth makes them” (1755) ↗.
  • The sentiment is far older than Franklin: it restates Virgil’s “Labor omnia vicit improbus” ↗ — steady work conquers all.
  • Quote databases repeat the Franklin credit but cite no source, e.g. The Quotations Page ↗, which lists only his dates.

As a short traditional aphorism — and one echoing a proverb some two millennia old — the line carries no meaningful copyright. The attribution to Franklin, however, is unverified.

Fact-check

The attribution problem

This is one of the most-shared “Franklin” lines online, yet it appears in none of his published works, letters or almanacs.

Repeated as Franklin’s on Goodreads, BrainyQuote and countless motivational sites, but with no citation. A search of the scholarly corpus — the Papers of Benjamin Franklin ↗ — returns no matching document, and Wikiquote ↗ does not list it among his sourced or even his misattributed quotations.

Context

Why it sticks to Franklin

Franklin built a personal brand on industry decades before the phrase ‘personal brand’ existed. Poor Richard’s Almanack hammered the same message every year from 1733 to 1758 — that steady effort beats talent and luck — so a punchy modern paraphrase about ‘energy and persistence’ slots naturally under his name.

Because the sentiment is authentically his, the misattribution is easy to forgive and hard to dislodge: readers recognize the idea as Franklin’s and assume the words are too. They are not — they are a 20th-century-flavored rewording of a proverb that runs from Virgil through the English ‘perseverance conquers all things.’

“All things are easy to Industry, all things difficult to Sloth.” — Franklin’s actual 1734 line

The attributed author

Why the line lands on Franklin

BF

Benjamin Franklin

1706–1790 · American printer, scientist and statesman

Benjamin Franklin (1706–1790) was a printer, scientist and one of the founding fathers of the United States, best known for Poor Richard’s Almanack and its aphorisms on thrift and industry. His genuine maxims — “Diligence overcomes Difficulties, Sloth makes them” and “All things are easy to Industry, all things difficult to Sloth” — made him the natural magnet for any later saying about the power of hard work. He is not, however, documented anywhere as writing the exact line “Energy and persistence conquer all things.”
