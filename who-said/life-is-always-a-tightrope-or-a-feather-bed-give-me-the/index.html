Life is always a tightrope or a feather bed. Give me the tightrope

Attributed to Edith Wharton 1862–1937 · American novelist & short-story writer
≈ Attribution status: Attributed

Quotation sites and Wikiquote nearly universally credit this line to Wharton, with Wikiquote dating it to a “Journal entry (March 1926)” — but no published edition of her diaries, letters, or other writing could be found to confirm the exact wording, so the primary source remains unconfirmed.

Life is always a tightrope or a feather bed. Give me the tightrope.

Quotation sites are split on the exact wording — some (Goodreads, AZQuotes) render it “Life is always either a tightrope or a feather bed,” others (BrainyQuote, Quotefancy) drop “either.” No primary source was found to settle which is original.

How we verified this

  • Widely repeated as Edith Wharton's own words on quotation sites such as Goodreads ↗ and Quotefancy ↗, none of which name a book, letter, or date.
  • Wikiquote ↗ is the only source found that offers any provenance, dating the line to a “Journal entry (March 1926)” — but it cites no published edition.
  • A direct full-text search of Wharton's 1925 writing manual, The Writing of Fiction ↗, found no occurrence of “tightrope”; the book's single “feather-bed” sits in an unrelated passage about small-town fiction (“sinking delectably into the feather-bed of the small town”), confirming the quoted line is not in this work.

The only provenance offered anywhere is an unsourced claim that this line comes from a Wharton journal entry of March 1926; without a published diary, letters volume, or scholarly edition to confirm that date and text, no publication date can be fixed, so rights status is left uncertain rather than assumed public domain.

Context

Why it mattered

This aphorism is one of the most widely shared Wharton quotations online, usually paired with her reputation as a writer who prized risk, discipline, and hard work over comfort and ease. That reputation is well documented across her published life and letters — but the tightrope line itself is not: it circulates almost entirely through quotation aggregators that cite no source, and the one site that offers any date, Wikiquote, points only to “a journal entry, March 1926” without naming the diary or edition it drew from.

About Edith Wharton

EW

Edith Wharton

1862–1937 · American novelist & short-story writer

Edith Wharton (1862–1937) was an American novelist and short-story writer best known for closely observed novels of Gilded Age New York society, including The House of Mirth (1905), Ethan Frome (1911), and The Age of Innocence (1920), for which she became the first woman to win the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction. She also wrote prolifically across letters, travel writing, and design criticism, and kept private diaries and journals that biographers have since drawn on for her unguarded voice.
