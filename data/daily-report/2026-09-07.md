## 5. Findings

### 5.1 `tools/review.js:267` hands the auditor a dead rights signal — corpus-wide, new today

`weekly-discovery` verified and quantified this, and did not fix it (`WEEKLY-DISCOVERY.md` forbids
editing `tools/`). In its own words:

> `tools/review.js:267` reads the record's rights as:
> ```js
> rights: (rec.meta && rec.meta.rights) || rec.rights || null,
> ```
> Records store rights at **`rec.source.rights`**. Measured across the corpus:
> - 2,045 records total
> - **1,385** carry `source.rights`
> - **0** carry `meta.rights`
> - **0** carry a top-level `rights`
>
> So the `rights` value handed to the auditor via `review.js args` is `null` for every record in the
> corpus, and wrongly `null` for the 1,385 that actually have one. […] Effect: the rights-honesty
> signal the audit is supposed to check arrives dead. It has presumably been dead for every run that
> used `review.js args`.

It names a worked example: `the-telegraph-is-like-a-very-very-long-cat-or-dog` is
`source.rights: "public-domain"` and renders a "Free to reuse" badge on the live page, yet arrived at
the auditor as `"rights": null`. The fix it proposes is one line — read `rec.source.rights` in that
fallback chain. New item **72**.

This does not mean 1,385 rights values are wrong; it means **no audit has ever checked them**. Sizing
the exposure is part of the work, not a prerequisite to the one-line fix.
