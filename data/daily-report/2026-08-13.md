The wave reported it and deliberately did not patch it. In its words:

The two rows it hit are worth naming because they show what the glyph asserts wrongly — the 1997

it is making an unrecognised tag render the neutral marker instead of the accusation. **→ decision 1.**

### 2. NEW — `schema.verdictNote` is a real override that no wave can reach, so `disambiguatingDescription` flattens "the wording evolved" into "Actually by X"

The wave's finding, reported not fixed:

> on *lazy person*, `Quotation.disambiguatingDescription` renders "Commonly misattributed to Bill
> Gates. **Actually by Clarence E. Bleicher.**" That is flatter than both the page prose and QI,
> which credit Bleicher with the *rough formulation* and say the wording evolved afterwards. The
> string is computed in `tools/template.js` from confidence + creditedTo + creator, and its only
> override (`schema.verdictNote`) is not in `DOSSIER_SCHEMA`, so no wave can emit it.

**Verified both halves.** `schema.verdictNote` is honoured at `tools/template.js:793` and lands on
`quotation.disambiguatingDescription` at `:857`; `grep -n verdictNote workflows/generate.js` returns
**nothing**. The escape hatch exists in the renderer and is unreachable from the record pipeline.

The wave's mitigation is sound and worth recording: *"`Quotation.text` on that node is Bleicher's
verbatim sentence, and the ClaimReview separately rates the popular wording **False**."* So this is
an overclaim in one string, not a false page. **→ decision 2.**
