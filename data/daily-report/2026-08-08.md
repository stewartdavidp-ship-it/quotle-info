## Findings

### 1. `mis-kind.js` renders the refutation ✕ on rows that refute nothing — 74 rows, measured

r34's most serious finding, **reported by five independent agents**, one of whom measured it rather
than asserting it:

> Tags including **"Later variant"**, **"vaudeville stock"**, **"also borrowed"**, **"Truncated"**,
> **"Context stripped"** and **"Mis-sourced"** match no `CONTEXT_TAG` pattern, so `kindForTag()`
> returns `''` and the row renders the default refuted **✕** — asserting *"this credit is false"*
> beside prose that denies no credit at all. On pages whose verdict is that the quote is **genuine**,
> the glyph contradicts the sentence printed next to it.

The measurement, and an explicit recommendation **against** the obvious fix:

> the participle test alone fires on **131 rows (3.21%)**, but that set includes person subjects
> ("Serena Williams | trimmed") where ✕ may be correct. Intersected with "subject is not a person" it
> narrows to **74 rows (1.81%)**, and all 40 sampled were unambiguously wording notes wearing a
> refutation mark. Recommend the intersected form only.

**Today's wave hit it again and worked around it by hand** — its words: *"`mis-kind.js` would have
stamped the burgundy ✕ ('this credit is refuted') on three rows whose prose states the real line.
Reworded the row scopes/tags so the derived marker matches the prose — no `tools/` change."* Two
consecutive waves have now paid for this in prose edits. r34 is careful to separate it from #380:
*"that decision was about the schema; this is a gap in the patterns. Both hold."*

### 2. The `--credited` guard compares last words, and nearly libelled Dr. Seuss

r34, live:

> `the-more-that-you-read` returned author **"Dr. Seuss (Theodor Seuss Geisel)"** against a batch
> author of **"Dr. Seuss"** — a name expansion, not a reassignment, on a **verified** record whose
> misattribution rows are about the wrong *book*. `leadName` compares last words (`"Geisel)"` vs
> `"Seuss"`), so `--credited` would have stamped *"falsely credited to Dr. Seuss"* on a genuine Dr.
> Seuss quote.

Caught by a human-style check, not by the tool. The same expansion **also split the author hub**,
which the hand-check would not have caught and `verify-corpus.js` did: the build aborted with
`dr-seuss` and `dr-seuss-theodor-seuss-geisel` both claiming one Wikipedia identity. The wave applied
the invariant's own remedy (2 v 2 tie → bare name wins) and authors went 860 → 859. The invariant
worked; the guard is what is broken.
