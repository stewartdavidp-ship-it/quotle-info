### F3 — `plain()` silently deletes any HTML entity missing from its table (#553, r40)

> `tools/template.js` `plain()` decodes named entities through `NAMED_ENTITIES` and maps an
> unrecognised one to a **single space**, which the following `\s+` collapse then erases. `copy` is
> not in the table, so a `source.rightsHolder` of *"the 2006 article © Guardian News & Media"*
> reaches `quotation.copyrightNotice` as *"the 2006 article Guardian News & Media"* — © gone from
> the machine-readable notice while the visible rights block renders correctly.

Measured: **11 of 1,727 records** carry `©` in `source.rightsHolder`, and **~40 named entities the
corpus actually uses are absent from the table** (`copy reg trade euro pound sect para bull`, the
arrows, the whole Greek block). The recorded fix is deliberately two-part:

> the table **plus a design change**: return the original `&name;` for an unknown entity instead of
> `' '`, so the next gap appears as visible garbage rather than disappearing. The silent-loss default
> is precisely why this went unseen for so long.

This is a JSON-LD correctness defect — a copyright notice that reads as unowned to a machine — and it
is the only finding on this list that touches what search engines read.

### F4 — Re-auditing is not idempotent in its verdict, and `parse-audit.js` drops the loser's findings (#550/#551, r39)

And the counter that would normally catch a truncated run reads wrong on a resume:
