## Findings

### 1. A generator defect has now blocked three consecutive waves — and yesterday's diagnosis of it was wrong

The C. S. Lewis *"Friendship is born at that moment…"* candidate was drawn and released by the
08-06 wave, by today's daily wave, and by r32. Today's wave gave the correct diagnosis, which is
**not** the one in yesterday's report:

> The queue text ends in a spaced ellipsis (`. . .`). `toRecord`'s
> `displayQuote = text.replace(/\s*\.\s*$/, '')` strips only the final period, yielding
> `... no one but myself . .` — a visibly broken quotation as the page's headline text and
> clipboard citation.

Yesterday's report called the stored text *"truncated mid-sentence"*. It is not. Verified in
`data/harvest-queue.json`: the stored quote is complete and ends in a deliberate ellipsis, exactly
as the book prints it. **The data is fine; the generator is broken.** That relocates the fix from
`harvest.js` (yesterday's decision item 1) to `workflows/generate.js:48`, `:310` and
`workflows/prep-wave.js:62` — three copies of the same regex, confirmed present.

r32's words: *"This is the third wave to hit it — decision item still open."*

The wave also established, separately, that the circulating wording is itself drifted. Against the
first American edition (Harcourt, Brace, 1960, ch. IV, p. 112) the sentence reads *"Friendship,
**I have said**, is born at **the** moment…"* — so even repaired it is an `in-copyright` record
that needs `quotationText` and `claimQuoteText` to differ.

### 2. `harvest.js unselect` ignores its slug argument and releases everything — a data-loss trap

Found by r32, documented in #369, **not fixed**:

> `cmdUnselect` (harvest.js:294) releases **every** selected candidate, filtered only by `--wave`.
> It looks slug-aware because callers have used it when exactly one candidate happened to be
> selected — both #358 earlier today and today's daily wave. Running `unselect <slug>` here would
> have wiped all 41 selections.

Two callers on 2026-08-06 passed a slug that was purely decorative and could not have known from
the output. r32 worked around it by sweeping first so only the corrupt candidate remained
`selected`, then `unselect --wave r32`. #369 wrote the safe order down but deliberately left the
code alone: *"changing a destructive command's semantics deserves its own review rather than riding
along with a docs bump."* That is the right call and it leaves a loaded gun in the toolbox.

### 3. The FAQ answer contradicts the ClaimReview rating on ~48 pages

r32's most serious generator finding, reported not applied:

> `template.js:610` — `verdictLead` derives from `q.confidence` alone and ignores
> `schema.claimRatingValue`, so the Berra ballpark page ships a ClaimReview rated **2/5 "Mostly

Confirmed in the tree — line 610 is `q.confidence === 'disputed' ? 'No. ' : …` with no reference to
`claimRatingValue`. This is the voice-assistant channel the site is explicitly built for, saying
one thing in prose and the opposite in structured data.
