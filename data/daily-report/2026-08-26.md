## 4. Findings

### 4.1 The middle-initial hub fork is now systematic, and it aborted two builds *(decision 9)*

r44 is blunt about it. In #656's own words:

> That's the **fourth consecutive wave** with a middle-initial fork (r38 Drucker · r42 Maslow +
> Frankl · r43 Covey-inside-a-record · r44 Porter + Covey). Systematic generator behaviour, not
> chance.

It cost r44 **two build aborts**, and the wave is careful that neither was a new phenomenon — both
were defects in the pre-flight checks it ran itself:

> 1. **The Covey hub fork was wave-vs-corpus, not within-wave.** All three of this wave's records
>    agreed on `stephen-r-covey`, so an in-batch fork check sees no disagreement — but r43 had
>    already established `stephen-covey`. **A fork check must group the whole corpus by
>    `schema.creator.sameAs`, not just the batch.**
> 2. **The Porter hero/creator mismatch I *did* check for — but ran the check before stamping
>    `creditedTo`**, so its `r.creditedTo && …` guard was never true. Pure ordering bug. **Run the
>    RPWW check after stamping.**

It also deviated from the documented invariant, deliberately and correctly: it repointed to
`stephen-covey` because that is the hub r43 had already published, where the invariant's "most
records" tiebreak "would have picked `stephen-r-covey` 3-to-2 and orphaned a live URL."

**The fix shipped correctly** — verified on `main`: the author hubs are `stephen-covey` (5 quotes)
and `stephen-m-r-covey`, correctly distinct people, and the remaining `stephen-r-covey` strings in
the corpus are inside external QuotePark URLs, not hub slugs.

**And r44 attached a warning to its own proposed detector, which is the most useful thing in the PR:**

> Grouping the corpus by `sameAs` alone reports ~13 "forks", of which **most are misattribution
> pages working exactly as designed** — the hub is the magnet (`sun-tzu`), the hero is the true
> author (Vegetius). The real invariant is **scoped** to records where `schema.creator` *is* the
> hub's own person. A detector without that scope would "fix" the site's core shape. Mine repointed
> 0 records so nothing was corrupted, but it should not have been trusted.

So the pre-flight this needs is a *scoped* corpus-wide fork check, not the loose one — and that
scope is the whole design of the fix.
