## 5. Findings

### 5.1 Today's wave did not ship, and the merge ordering is why — NEW

This is the headline. It is nobody's mistake and every routine followed its doc.

- #752 opened 07:38 UTC. Its `verify` check went **green at 07:39:34**. Its base was `dd968ef`, the
  same `main` every other open PR was based on. It was mergeable.
- The 10:45 merge pass merged **#751, #753, #754** — three log shards, one line each — then recorded,
  in its own shard (`2026-09-03T10-45-39-152Z-daily-merge.jsonl`):

  > `merged #751 #753 #754 · re-poll 1/4: 1 PR(s) with mergeability not yet computed — waiting 6s ·`
  > `[…×4…] · WAIT: #752 — mergeabi`

- Under branch protection's `strict: true`, **each merge invalidates GitHub's mergeability
  computation for every other open PR**. The pass re-polled four times at 6 s — a **24-second** budget
  — then correctly applied the gate's `WAIT` rule ("leave it; next run picks it up") and stopped.

The gate now reads `REBUILD  #752 — 3 commit(s) behind main and rebuilds 37 derived file(s)`. So
tomorrow's 07:00 pass must merge `main` in and rebuild, and per `DAILY-MERGE.md` step 3 it must **not**
merge on the same pass — meaning today's five records land on **09-05 at the earliest**. Two days'
lag, bought by merging three files that contain no content.

**The asymmetry is the point.** `merge-run.js:56-58` records that the staleness demotion "now applies
only to PRs that actually rebuild derived output" — so a log-shard PR is *not* demoted by an earlier
merge, and a wave PR *is*. Merging content first therefore costs nothing and merging log shards first
costs a full rebuild and a day. The planner picks the first `MERGE` in PR-number order, and #751
simply sorted below #752.

*A longer re-poll budget would not have fixed this* — it would have returned `REBUILD` rather than
`unknown`, which at best lets the pass rebuild #752 today and merge it tomorrow. Only content-first
ordering ships it the same day. See item **64**.
