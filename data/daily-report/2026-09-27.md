## 5. Findings

### 5.1 Blocker, caught and fixed by the wave itself — a false "Verified" rating

Record 2 *"published a machine-readable 'Verified' rating on a wording Lewis did not write."*
`confidence: verified` rendered `ClaimReview.reviewRating` "Verified" against a claim *the page's own
prose corrects*. Lewis wrote *"one **man**"*, and the familiar ending belongs to a different sentence
in the same chapter. Downgraded to `disputed`, rating now "Mostly False", label *"Not quite — Lewis
wrote 'one man', and the ending is from another sentence"*. The page now cross-links the corpus's
existing `friendship-is-born-at-that-moment-when-one-man-says-to`, which independently reached the
same conclusion (*"often spliced together into a hybrid Lewis never wrote"*).

**This is the system working.** The audit stage caught, before publication, precisely the class of
error the whole pipeline exists to prevent.

### 5.2 NEW — a name-spelling mismatch silently suppresses the creator *and* publishes a false sentence

The wave's own words:

> I wrote `schema.creatorName: "C. S. Lewis"` while `answer.authorName` was `"C.S. Lewis"`. On a
> `disputed` page the creator guard requires those to match exactly, so record 2 shipped
> `creator: null` plus a *false* `disambiguatingDescription` ("Commonly misattributed to C.S. Lewis.
> The true author is not established") — flatly contradicting its own prose. … the failure is silent
> and the only symptom is a missing optional field: a wave that spells the author two ways in one
> record loses the creator the CLAUDE.md RULE asks for, and gains a false sentence. A
> `validate-records` check comparing `schema.creator.name` to `answer.authorName` would catch it
> mechanically. **No generator change made here** — reporting it, per the procedure.

Two things make this worth a number of its own (**98**) rather than folding into **92**:

1. **It falsifies 92's safety argument.** Item 92 rests on *"`creatorOk` suppresses them at render so
   **no page is wrong**"*. On a `disputed` page that is not true — suppression swaps in a
   misattribution claim about a person who is genuinely the author.
2. **It is not the detector #948 rejected.** That precedent (*"Duplicating a render guard as a flag
   produces a worse version of a tool we already have"*) was about a corpus **detector**. A
   `validate-records` check is a pre-commit gate on record *input*, which is where the two spellings
   diverge; the render guard cannot see the mistake, only its consequence.

It also touches a CLAUDE.md RULE directly: the Schema.org `creator` requirement is silently unmet
whenever this fires.
