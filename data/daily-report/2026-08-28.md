### 5.4 A false-credit defect that reached production, its narrowest possible fix, and 96 more like it *(decision 48)*

r46 shipped a page whose ClaimReview rated *"John McTiernan said…"* as False — a claim nobody makes —
while **Christopher Columbus**, the attribution people actually search, appeared nowhere in the
structured data. The cause, in #687's words:

> QI's conclusion read: *"crafted by screenwriter Larry Ferguson and **not** Christopher Columbus"*.
> Treating every mentioned name as "credited" classified Columbus as credited, leaving McTiernan —
> merely the director who *disclosed* the fabrication — as the only "other", so he became the magnet.

The fix is deliberately minimal and the reasoning is worth reading, because a fuller version was
built, measured and **rejected**: it moved 139 magnets of 544 multi-person articles, *"fixing some and
breaking others… at that volume there's no way to separate fixes from regressions."* The shipped
version moves **4 of 544** — two provably right, two shifting between equally arbitrary picks.

The new `validate-records.js` gate was calibrated over three passes from 123 flags to **2** — *"a gate
that cries wolf 60 times gets ignored, which is worse than no gate"* — and immediately found two real
defects: `good-artists-copy-great-artists-steal` (fixed: `creditedTo` was Steve Jobs under a label
reading "Not Picasso") and `laws-are-like-sausages`, caught **while still queued**, which would have
published *"John Godfrey Saxe said…"* rated False — the true author accused of the false credit.

**What remains is the open part:** #686 reports *"118 synced candidates come from articles tagging 3+
people, 96 still queued"*. Each now carries an `AMBIGUOUS MAGNET` note, because that pick becomes
`creditedTo` and, in #687's words, *"asserts a false credit against someone by name."*
